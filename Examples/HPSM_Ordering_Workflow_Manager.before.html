Level of Care Reference
Determining the appropriate transport level for HPSM members
V1 • Rev. 04/2026
Use this reference to determine the correct level of care before calling for transport. The level of care dictates whether you call Royal Dispatch directly or go through HPSM.
BLSBasic Life Support

Call Royal Dispatch directly. Patient requires basic medical monitoring during transport.

  • Stable patients needing stretcher transport
  • Oxygen administration (nasal cannula, simple mask)
  • Basic vitals monitoring (BP, pulse, SpO2)
  • IV maintenance (no new starts or medication pushes)
  • Non-emergency transfers between facilities

Common examples: Dialysis transfers, SNF to hospital for routine appointments, stable post-surgical patients, discharge transports requiring a stretcher.

ALSAdvanced Life Support

Call Royal Dispatch directly. Patient requires advanced interventions beyond BLS capabilities.

  • Cardiac monitoring (12-lead ECG interpretation)
  • IV medication administration
  • Advanced airway management
  • Blood glucose monitoring and treatment
  • Patients with unstable vitals or risk of deterioration

Common examples: Chest pain patients, acute stroke transfers, patients on IV drips (non-vasopressor), sepsis patients needing higher-level monitoring.

CCTCritical Care Transport

Call Royal Dispatch directly. Patient requires ICU-level care and monitoring during transport.

  • Ventilator management (mechanical ventilation)
  • Vasopressor / vasoactive drip management
  • Invasive hemodynamic monitoring (arterial lines)
  • Chest tube management
  • Patients requiring 1:1 RN or RT attendance

Common examples: ICU-to-ICU transfers, patients on multiple pressors, ventilated patients, ECMO transports, post-cardiac arrest stabilization transfers.

WC / SVWheelchair & Stretcher Van / Gurney

Call HPSM first to arrange transport. Patient does NOT require medical monitoring during transport.

  • Patient can sit upright in a wheelchair for transport
  • Patient needs a stretcher/gurney but no medical care en route
  • No oxygen, IV, cardiac monitoring, or medication needs
  • Non-emergency, routine appointments and discharges

Common examples: Doctor appointments, dialysis (ambulatory), routine discharges of stable patients who cannot use personal vehicles, SNF transfers without medical needs.

Important: HPSM NEMT Authorization

For Wheelchair and Stretcher Van transports, SMMC must order through HPSM so that HPSM covers the cost. Only if HPSM cannot fulfill the transport should you escalate to Royal Dispatch. Refer to the front-side workflow for the complete decision tree and billing designations.
