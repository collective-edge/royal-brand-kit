Dispatch report

Morning booking puts discharge rides on time.

Two hospitals booked next-day discharge rides before 10:00 for six weeks. Late pickups fell at both. The rule applies to every partner hospital from 1 December.

For

Case management leads

From

Royal Dispatch

Period

1 Oct to 11 Nov 2026

Summary.

Case managers at two hospitals sent next-day discharge requests through the ordering page before 10:00. Dispatch confirmed each ride within 15 minutes with a unit and a pickup window.

With the morning list in hand, dispatch planned units against it. Fewer patients waited past their window, and fewer units arrived before the patient was ready.

What case managers reported.

Case managers asked for the pickup window in the confirmation itself. Every confirmation now names the unit and the window. The crew calls the nursing station 20 minutes before arrival.

Risks.

Basic life support windows are limited after 15:00 on weekdays. A late request at that level may move to the next morning.

If a discharge moves, call dispatch. The unit holds for 15 minutes past the window, then returns to service.

Next steps.

  1. 1Dispatch staffs one dispatcher on the ordering page from 07:00 to 10:00.
  2. 2Every partner hospital books by 10:00 from 1 December.
  3. 3Dispatch logs any ride confirmed late, with the reason.
  4. 4Royal reviews on-time pickup again on 15 January 2027.

Method.

A ride is on time when the unit arrives at the nursing station inside the confirmed pickup window. Arrival is the time the crew marks on scene. The window is the one named in the confirmation.

The figures before the test cover the six weeks to 30 September at the same two hospitals. Rides cancelled by the hospital before the window opened are left out of both periods.

Limits.

Two hospitals is a small group. The January review covers every partner hospital for the first full month under the rule.
